Frequently asked questions

Twenty questions that come in often enough to be worth answering in one place: ordering, downloads, refunds, substitutions, allergens, printing, privacy and accessibility.

If your question is not here, write to hello@thistledownkitchen.com or use the contact form. We read everything and answer on weekdays, usually within two business days. Questions that keep coming back end up on this page.

US customary throughout: cups, tablespoons, teaspoons, ounces, pounds and degrees Fahrenheit. Where a weight matters more than a volume, which in baking is most of the time, the weight comes first in ounces with the cup measure after it in brackets. Flour is always given by weight, because a cup of it can vary by nearly two ounces depending on how it got into the cup.

Usually, and every recipe has a substitutions section that says which swaps work straight across, which ones change the timing or texture, and which ones do not work at all. Every recipe carries an allergen line naming the major allergens present in the ingredients as written — wheat and gluten, dairy, egg, soy, tree nuts, peanuts, sesame, fish and shellfish. That line describes the recipe, not your shopping. Manufacturers change formulations without notice, so if you are cooking for someone with an allergy you must read the label on every package you actually buy. If you are not certain about a product, do not use it.

They are estimates and we label them as estimates. They are calculated from standard ingredient databases using the quantities in the recipe divided by the stated number of servings. They are not measured in a laboratory and they will shift with brands, with how big you cut the portions and with any substitution you make. They are there for rough comparison only and they are not dietary advice. For advice about your own diet, speak to a qualified professional.

Advertising, privacy and access

Advertising and the two products in the shop are what pay for the testing, the photography and the hosting. Advertisement slots are labelled Advertisement so you can tell at a glance what is ours and what is not, they are kept well away from buttons and links so you do not hit one by accident, and they never appear on the shop, the product page, the order page, the contact page or any policy page. Advertisers have no say in what we publish. See the Advertising and Affiliate Disclosure.

No. When we name a piece of equipment, nobody is paying us to name it and there is no commission attached to any link on this site. If that ever changes, the disclosure will sit directly next to the link in the body text where you cannot miss it, not buried in the footer.

Two things, and only if you asked for them. If you bought something, we use it to send the download and to answer you if the delivery goes wrong. If you signed up for the Saturday note, we use it to send that note. We do not sell it, rent it or share it with anyone for their own marketing. Every note has an unsubscribe link and it works immediately. The full picture is in the Privacy Policy.

Go to Your Privacy Choices and use the control there. No account is needed. We also honour the Global Privacy Control browser signal automatically — if your browser or extension sends it, you are opted out on arrival and the cookie banner does not appear at all.

We build to WCAG 2.2 Level AA and in several places we go past it: body text is set at 19 pixels, the contrast of ordinary text against its background is at least 7:1 rather than the 4.5:1 the standard requires, every interactive target is at least 44 by 44 pixels, links in body text are always underlined, and there is a skip link and a visible focus outline on everything you can reach with a keyboard. If something still does not work for you, tell us which page and what happened. See the Accessibility Statement.

Please link to it rather than copying it. If you want to reproduce a recipe in full — in a community newsletter, a class handout, a church cookbook — write and ask. We almost always say yes for non-commercial use and we just want to know where it is going and to be credited with a link.
