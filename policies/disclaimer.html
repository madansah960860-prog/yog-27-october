Disclaimer

The limits of what Thistledown Kitchen publishes, and where you should be getting advice instead.

Last updated October 27, 2026.

This is a cooking site

Thistledown Kitchen publishes recipes and cooking guidance for general interest. It is not a source of medical, nutritional, dietary or food-safety advice for your individual circumstances, and nothing on this site should be used as a substitute for advice from a qualified professional who knows your situation.

We make no health claims

Nothing on this site is intended to diagnose, treat, cure or prevent any disease or health condition, and nothing here should be read as suggesting that any food or recipe will do so. We do not claim that any recipe published here will improve any health outcome, affect body weight, or change any medical condition. If you read something on this site as a health claim, that is not what we meant and we would like to know so we can rewrite it.

If you have a medical condition, are pregnant, are taking medication that interacts with food, or are following a diet prescribed by a doctor or a registered dietitian, follow their guidance rather than ours.

Nutrition figures are estimates

Where we publish nutrition information it is an estimate, calculated from standard ingredient databases using the quantities in the recipe divided by the stated number of servings. It is not laboratory measured. Real values shift with the brands you buy, with how large you cut the portions, with how much of a marinade is actually absorbed, and with any substitution you make. Treat the figures as rough comparison between recipes and nothing more. They are not dietary advice.

Allergens

Every recipe carries an allergen line naming the major allergens present in the ingredients as written: wheat and gluten, dairy, egg, soy, tree nuts, peanuts, sesame, fish and shellfish. That line describes our recipe, not your shopping.

If you are cooking for someone with a food allergy, you must read the label on every package you actually buy. Manufacturers change formulations without notice, shared production lines create cross-contact risks that no recipe can anticipate, and a product that was safe last year may not be this year. If you are not certain about an ingredient, do not use it. Thistledown Kitchen cannot take responsibility for an allergic reaction.

Food safety

Cooking involves heat, sharp tools and raw ingredients. You are responsible for safe practice in your own kitchen. In particular:

  • Cook meat, poultry, fish and eggs to safe internal temperatures measured with a thermometer, not by appearance.
  • Do not leave cooked food at room temperature for more than two hours, or one hour above 90°F.
  • Raw flour and raw eggs both carry a real risk and should not be eaten.
  • Keep raw and cooked foods apart, and wash hands, boards and knives between them.
  • When a stored food is in any doubt at all — smell, appearance, time — throw it away.

Our storage times assume a refrigerator at or below 40°F and a freezer at 0°F. They are guidance for planning, not a guarantee about a particular container in a particular appliance.

Results vary

Ovens run hot and cold, pans conduct differently, altitude changes boiling and baking, and ingredients differ by season and supplier. We test everything we publish, but we cannot guarantee that a recipe will behave identically in your kitchen. Where a step is sensitive to these variables, the recipe says so and tells you what to look for rather than only how long to wait.
